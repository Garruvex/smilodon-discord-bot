# Participation improvements: implementation and rollout

Workstreams 1–5, 2026-10-15. Implemented locally; live shadow evaluation, threshold calibration and human acceptance are pending. No production setting has been enabled by this work. [Workstream 6](relationship-memory-fading.md) is the separate committed memory change.

## Behavior

Optional continuation and spontaneous replies require a structured decision provider or its utility fallback. A valid decision supplies recipient evidence IDs, separate recipient and target confidence, a labelled contribution with its source and recipient, and an opening assessment. Failed or invalid providers try the utility fallback, then abstain. Recipient, target and overall confidence retain a conservative 0.7 floor; these self-ratings are not calibrated probabilities. Unavailable and failed outcomes are distinguishable; skipped evaluations are recorded separately. Direct requests retain their usual path and receive only a short restraint instruction, without a new decision call.

The application checks explicit reply links and mentions before names, then eligible observed display names and existing audience-filtered memory aliases. Unresolved or ambiguous names abstain. Third-person references to a bot name do not trigger the name-address path. A question mark, second-person pronoun or shared topic cannot override an explicit other recipient or an unclear local recipient. Spontaneous group contributions remain possible with a specific eligible contribution and a clear opening.

An implicit exchange window starts at the first confirmed bot delivery answering a newly engaged human message and lasts 120 seconds. Each human source can renew it only once. Extra bot output cannot renew it or recruit someone who did not engage after the last accepted reply. Human interleaving, a new recipient/branch or a closing cancels implicit continuation; explicit replies remain usable. Reply branches are reconstructed from the bounded eligible conversation episode, including implicit same-person links. If the necessary history is missing, the application abstains rather than inventing a relationship.

Acknowledgment-only, repetition, generic advice, speculative help, topic hijacking, stale information and no contribution are rejected at every score. Eligible labels include a specific opinion, relevant joke, personal reaction, useful observation or answer. The accepted candidate guides generation. A deterministic post-check suppresses pure acknowledgments and exact repeated bot text before delivery. It does not prove semantic alignment for arbitrary paraphrases: persona fit, factual fidelity and whether a contribution is welcome still require model replay and human review.

Held thoughts are bounded and scoped by guild, channel, branch, recipient, topic and settings. They can be reused only when a new valid decision reassesses the same candidate. Holding them does not refresh factual evidence or authorize a send. Failed decisions, removed source context, forgetting and settings/access changes clear or invalidate stale state. Discord content edits and single/bulk deletions invalidate ephemeral branches immediately; tombstones prevent a stale persisted episode load from restoring them. Routing corrections such as “I am talking to someone else” close continuation and are excluded from member-memory proposals and dedicated extraction.

## Settings and compatibility

`chat.participation.spontaneousThreshold` and `continuationThreshold` are the effective score settings. Their existing baseline defaults remain 0.75 and 0.5; labels and recipient/opening checks run first. The public thought-threshold control is removed. When parsing an old configuration without the new threshold, the former live thought threshold maps to `max(0.75, oldThreshold)` for spontaneous participation; an explicit new value takes precedence. Parsed configurations carry the new values, so changing a deprecated thought threshold later does not change admission.

The former active shadow-thought hook could veto spontaneous replies. For old configurations where it applied, migration pauses spontaneous participation in the affected existing allow-listed channels once, rather than silently activating speech when that veto is removed. Review the channel override and explicitly opt in or reset it when ready. New configurations and those with an explicit new threshold do not receive this compatibility pause.

Optional continuation has a minimum 15-second interval between optional text deliveries, branch-specific exchange counters and a channel aggregate quota. Failed/suppressed sends do not consume delivered quotas. Reservations coordinate concurrent branches. Balanced/lively retain the former 10-per-exchange and 30-per-channel/10-minute caps; quiet uses a 30-second interval and 6/15 caps, alongside its existing quieter spontaneous policy. Advanced overrides remain available. These are local implementation limits awaiting replay/pilot review, not calibrated social scores. The proposed 5/12 experiment caps and any marginal-score band have not been selected as new live defaults.

`chat.participation.messagesPerDecision` (the participation control "Messages per decision") accepts 1-20 and defaults to 5. Each exact channel or thread accumulates unique eligible human messages before optional continuation or spontaneous evaluation. Reaching the count opens the existing four-second quiet / ten-second maximum coalescing window; it does not promise a reply. The batch is consumed when handed off for evaluation, even if later policy or resource limits suppress that attempt. Individual fragments expire after two minutes, and two minutes of inactivity clears the batch; there is no forced evaluation below the count. Bots do not count. Direct mentions, name-addressed requests and explicit replies to the bot bypass accumulation and invalidate pending optional work, including replies with the reply ping disabled. Settings/access changes and removed source context invalidate stale batches.

`participation.controls.spontaneous-threshold` and `continuation-threshold` expose percentages. `participation.continuation-limits` contains interval and caps. `participation.status` explains the provider dependency and reports the passive scope. All new controls and explanations are translated into English, Traditional Chinese and Japanese.

`chat.participation.observationChannelIds` selects passive observation and optional participation scope. When absent in an older configuration, it uses the existing nonempty chatbot channel list. An empty legacy chatbot list no longer grants unrestricted passive reading. An explicit empty observation list permits none. Only exact channel/thread IDs match: selecting a parent does not authorize its threads. The ordinary channel picker lists server text channels; use `participation.thread-scope` with an accessible thread ID to explicitly add or remove a thread. That action checks the server and does not enable global participation controls. Owners retain explicit-request access but receive no passive scope or role bypass. Channel history and context must be enabled; memory-disabled channels remain excluded. A spontaneous reply also requires its separate spontaneous allow-list.

Daily summaries and history scans retain their separate explicit channel selections and memory-mode checks. Their scope is independent of optional social participation. Guild memories can inform an authorized response under their existing audience rules, but do not grant access to another channel. Normal settings display daily-summary and history-scan channels separately.

## Evidence and verification

Decision review records contain outcome, recipient evidence IDs/confidence, contribution label, opening, target, effective policy/limits, latency and proposed action. They omit thought text and message content. Delivery records distinguish confirmed sends from previews or suppressions. Source exclusion removes linked review records and prevents stale in-flight records from restoring them. Review storage remains bounded to 500 records and 30 days. Forgetting clears the guild's review cache because records may link multiple participants; in-flight stale decisions are invalidated too.

Run the deterministic policy comparison without network access:

```powershell
npm.cmd run eval:character-replay -- --participation-fixture tests/fixtures/participation-replay.json --output node_modules/.test-tmp/participation-review.json
```

The synthetic fixture includes Traditional Chinese closings, 50–60-second delays, interleaving, explicit replies, name/alias ambiguity, missing/future evidence, missing image context, provider unavailability and a welcome spontaneous group joke. It compares the former score/null-admission policy with the new policy on identical supplied contexts and recorded decisions. It deliberately holds the recipient classifier constant to isolate policy; it is not an end-to-end comparison with an older deployed revision. Recorded-provider call counts and local replay timings are not production model latency measurements.

Regression tests additionally cover branch renewal, cross-branch concurrent reservations, quiet presets, threshold monotonicity/migration, provider failure/fallback, source deletion restoration, in-flight context cancellation, generated acknowledgment suppression, owner/empty-list/thread boundaries, history fetch prevention and review forgetting. Real-model calls are already timed in decision review metadata.

Before live activation: review upgraded scope and mapped thresholds, inspect deployment configuration, run paired real-model replay on consented examples, label recipient accuracy and interruptions, include four-to-six-reply exchanges and multi-message/image cases, and agree on a marginal-score band if that experiment is desired. Run shadow evaluation in one selected channel; approve a live pilot only after reviewers find welcome contributions and no explicit-other-recipient or channel-boundary violations. No live pilot or human preference result is claimed here.

## Local verification results

Final verification on 2026-10-15: 4,348 tests passed across 322 files; seven environment-dependent suites were skipped. Type checking, the production build, changed-file ESLint and migration normalization checks passed. The deterministic participation replay matched all 28 labelled cases, including a welcome group contribution. Whole-repository lint retains the previously recorded unrelated/generated-file failures; no global lint pass is claimed. PostgreSQL execution, real-model comparison, live shadow review and human pilot acceptance remain pending.
